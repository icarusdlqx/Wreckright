# Wreckright: first-time player test

## Invitation to share

I’m testing a browser tactics game about commanding a small mech company. I’d value 30–40 minutes with someone who has not played it before. This is a test of the game, not your ability. Please say what you expect to happen, where you hesitate, and what surprises you. Stop whenever you wish.

Use the supplied review build or offline file. Start with a fresh browser profile so your first-time experience is preserved. Please do not use a walkthrough. Headphones are optional; note whether sound was on. We will not record your screen or voice unless you agree first.

## Player activities

1. From the opening screen, learn enough to select machines, move, attack and pause.
2. Start a regular-difficulty company. Choose and complete its first contract, or stop when you would naturally quit.
3. Explain what came home and what needs attention. Prepare a lance for the next mission.
4. In the workshop, make a weapon change you think will help. Explain how you decided where it could fit. You may cancel the change.
5. If time permits, try the limited-tonnage survey operation. Decide what to send and why.

Afterwards, please answer:

- What was the most enjoyable decision?
- Where did the game do something different from what you expected?
- What do Guard, Attack and Move mean to you now?
- Could you tell which weapons fit and which parts you actually owned?
- Did the faction differences change a decision, or mainly change the appearance?
- Was anything hard to see, hear, read or control?
- Would you voluntarily play another mission? Why?

## Facilitator notes — keep separate from the player instructions

Recruit 5–8 first-time players across tactics experience levels. Include at least one touch-device session if touch is part of the intended release. Record a participant code, device/browser, input method, prior tactics experience, build revision and sound/graphics settings. Do not collect unnecessary personal information.

Let the player act. Ask “What are you trying to do?” before offering help. Record any help verbatim and when it occurred; an assisted success is not an uncoached success. Separate observed behavior from your interpretation. Capture a screenshot or use the game’s feedback export only with the player’s agreement. Existing local journal data covers a bounded early session, so use the observation sheet for the full test.

| Observation | Record |
| --- | --- |
| First purposeful order | Elapsed time; how the player discovered it |
| Pause understood | Used voluntarily? Orders issued while paused? |
| Orders predictable | Any old movement returning or confusing attack approach? |
| Sensor contact understood | Track versus confirmed target; attempted action |
| Lance choice | Recognizes tonnage, berth limit and reserve status? |
| Weapon fitting | Recognizes boxes, mount category, spare stock and replacement preview? |
| Consequences | Explains repair cost, injuries, XP and guaranteed rewards versus salvage? |
| Legibility | Occlusion, overlapping panels, missed alerts, tiny controls |
| Pacing and enjoyment | Idle stretches, repetitive commands, meaningful choices |
| Return intent | Wants another mission without prompting? Reason |

Suggested decision rules: address any lost-save, incorrect inventory, dead-end or misleading-order issue immediately. Revise any core step that repeatedly requires explanation. Treat enthusiasm and return intent as qualitative evidence, not a statistical claim from a small sample. Keep the first test about clarity and one complete campaign loop before expanding content again.
